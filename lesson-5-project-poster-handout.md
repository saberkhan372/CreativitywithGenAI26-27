## Part C — Run and build (35 min)

Run your test. Save every prompt and output. Then build **one page.**

### The poster must contain all six zones

| Zone | What goes in it |
|---|---|
| **1 · Question** | The question you tested, in one sentence |
| **2 · Method** | What you changed, what you held constant, how many runs |
| **3 · Evidence** | The actual outputs — screenshots or verbatim text, side by side |
| **4 · Claim** | The full four-part stem, including the fourth clause |
| **5 · Limits** | What your test does **not** establish. At least two things. |
| **6 · Authorship** | What you decided vs. what the model produced |

**There is no separate write-up.** If it is not on the page, it was not submitted.
Zone 4 and Zone 3 must sit close enough that a reader can check one against the other
without scrolling or turning anything over.

**Format:** Canva, Google Slides (one slide, landscape), or paper. Your choice.

## How this is assessed

| | What earns it |
|---|---|
| **The question is testable** | Small, specific, answerable with what you had today |
| **One variable** | You changed one thing and can say what you held constant |
| **Evidence is present and real** | Actual outputs, verbatim or screenshotted — not described |
| **The claim is bounded** | All four clauses, and the fourth one is specific |
| **Limits are honest** | At least two, and they are about *your* test, not AI in general |
| **Authorship is named** | Clear what you decided and what the model produced |

Notice what is **not** on this list: whether your result was interesting, whether the
model performed well, or whether you were right. A careful test with a boring result
is a complete success. A dramatic finding you cannot support is not.

## What Unit 1 was actually about

Three machines in five days: **ELIZA** (rules, no data — felt like understanding,
wasn't), **BookBot** (data, no rules — fluent sentences that meant nothing), and
**LLMs** (learned prediction at scale — fluent, sometimes right, always confident).

**Fluency is not accuracy**, and the meaning you feel in a machine's output is
usually the meaning *you* brought to it.
