Lesson 2 · Part BHandout Part B
Naming what you found

The six moves

Mirror

Reflects your own words back at you.

Pronoun swap

"my" → "your". "I" → "you".

Keyword hook

Reacts to one word: mother, dream, sad.

Generic prompt

"Tell me more" when nothing matches.

Question deflect

Answers a question with a question.

Echo fragment

Drops part of your sentence into a template.

That is the entire program. There is nothing underneath.

Lesson 2 · Part BTick these on your handout
Break the illusion · 10 minutes

Make it fail on purpose.

Design one input a person would handle easily and ELIZA cannot.

Not gibberish. Nonsense is too easy and proves almost nothing. Aim at what it lacks: memory of what you said three turns ago, a fact about the world, a number, a contradiction it should notice.

Lesson 2 · Part CHandout Part C
Before we go further

Two cautions

Anthropomorphism

Feeling understood is evidence about you, not about the program. ELIZA has no model of you, no memory, no idea you exist.

Sycophancy

A system that agreeably reflects you back feels supportive while being useless — or harmful. A modern chatbot is better at this than ELIZA. That makes it more convincing, not more trustworthy.

Neither is a therapist, a confidant, or a friend. If something in your life is heavy, the answer is a person — a counselor, a family member, a teacher. Including me.
