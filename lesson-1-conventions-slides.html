What happened

Nobody has ever written those rules.

  • Every rule you wrote needs another rule underneath it.
  • Every rule you wrote has a photo that breaks it.
  • Face unlock, spam filters, autocomplete — none were built this way.

So how were they built?

Lesson 1 · HistoryBletchley Park, Buckinghamshire
1950 · a different question

Can a machine think?

Turing suggested we stop asking what is going on inside a machine — and judge it instead by how it behaves in a conversation.

Hold on to that. Someone is about to take him up on it — and it will turn out that behaving convincingly in conversation is much easier than it sounds.

Lesson 1 · History“Computing Machinery and Intelligence”, 1950
1966 · taking Turing up on it

ELIZA

A program with no learning, no data, and no memory — rules all the way down — that people confided in anyway.

Person
My boyfriend made me come here.
ELIZA
Your boyfriend made you come here?

Say this out loud. Feeling understood is evidence about you, not about the program. A modern chatbot is better at this than ELIZA — more convincing, not more trustworthy. Neither is a therapist, a confidant, or a friend.

Lesson 1 · First lookWe take it apart properly next time
The answer to the cat question

Nobody writes the rules.

You could not write rules for "cat." So don't. Show a machine examples instead — and it works in about three minutes.

What you did

Two classes. Around thirty examples each. Train. Test. You never wrote a single rule.

Then you broke it

Show it something it has never seen. It answers anyway — confidently, and wrong.

Remember that last part. It comes back every day of this unit.

Lesson 1 · Activityteachablemachine.withgoogle.com
Three machines, two kinds

Two ways to build

Rules a person wrote

The Bombe · ELIZA

  • A person specifies every step
  • You can read it afterward
  • Odd input → it breaks, or stalls

Rules fitted from examples

Teachable Machine

  • You supply examples, not steps
  • Mostly you cannot read it
  • Odd input → confident nonsense

That last row is the difference that matters all semester.

Lesson 1 · The distinction
Class conventions · all semester

How we will work

1
Log the exact thing.

Prompt and output verbatim. A retyped output is not evidence.

2
Observation before interpretation.

"One sentence was false" ≠ "it lied." Say which you mean.

3
One variable at a time.

Two changes at once tells you nothing about either.

4
Name the authorship.

What you decided, what it produced. Every time.

5
Consent and privacy.

No real names, faces, or private details into any tool. Ever.

6
Participation is not obligation.

Observing, recording, questioning, declining — all full participation.
