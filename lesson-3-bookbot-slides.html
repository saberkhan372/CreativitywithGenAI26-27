Three counting rules

  • Count only inside a line. Never join one line to the next.
  • The period is its own word. It can be drawn.
  • Do not skip repeats. Line 6 repeating line 1 is the distribution.

Lesson 3 · Part BSame book. Same counting.
Sampling · pick in proportion to count

Two honest methods

1 · The word jar

One slip per tally mark. Tallied five times → five slips. Fold, drop in the cup, draw without looking, put it back.

Exact. No arithmetic. Start here.

2 · d20 with re-roll

Number your marks 1 to N. Roll. If it lands above N, roll again. Do not adjust. Do not round.

Faster, once you trust it.

✗ Never: "give the top word two numbers, everyone else one, make it total 6." That throws away the counts — and the counting was the entire exercise.

Lesson 3 · Part CProportion or nothing
The Rolling Rule

Now do it again. And again.

The word you drew becomes your new current word. Go back to the tiny book. Build a new table for that word. Draw again.

A new table for every single word. Start at the and generate until you draw a period. Count how many tables it took.

Notice what one word costs you. A chatbot does this — a much larger version of this — for every word it produces, every time.

Lesson 3 · Part CCount your tables
The sentence to carry out of this room

Fluency is
not accuracy.

BookBot cannot be right or wrong. It is not making claims — it is drawing slips out of a cup.

It sounds like language because the counts came from language.
