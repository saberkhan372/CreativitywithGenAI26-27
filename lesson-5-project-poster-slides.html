Lesson 5 of 570 minutes · Unit 1 assessment
Warm-up · 8 minutes

Which one can you check?

Two posters. Both make a claim. Only one lets you verify it.

Ask of each

  • What exactly is the claim?
  • Where is the evidence?
  • Could you check it, right now, from what is on the page?

The difference is usually

One shows the actual output. The other describes the output. A description is not evidence — it is a claim about evidence.

Lesson 5 · Part AHandout Part A
Design · 10 minutes

Shrink it until it fits.

✗ Too big for today

"Is AI biased?"

Not testable in 35 minutes. Not testable in a year.

✓ Testable today

"Does this model write a different job description if I change only the name?"

One variable. Everything else held.

Write down before you run anything: your question, the one thing you will change, everything you will hold constant, and your prediction.

Lesson 5 · Part BHandout Part B
Build · 35 minutes · one page

Six zones. All on the page.

1

Question

What you tested, in one sentence.

2

Method

What changed, what was held, how many runs.

3

Evidence

The actual outputs. Screenshots or verbatim — side by side.

4

Claim

The full four-part stem. Fourth clause included.

5

Limits

At least two things your test does not establish.

6

Authorship

What you decided vs. what the model produced.

Zones 3 and 4 go next to each other. A reader must be able to check the claim against the evidence without turning anything over. There is no separate write-up — if it is not on the page, it was not submitted.

Lesson 5 · Part CCanva · Slides · paper — your choice
Gallery walk · 12 minutes

Review two. Then look again at yours.

For each poster you visit

  • Is the claim checkable from what is shown?
  • Are the limits specific, or generic?
  • What is the strongest thing here?
  • One question you would ask this person.

Then, about your own

After seeing two others — what is the one thing you would change about yours?

Write it down. This is the part that sticks.

Lesson 5 · Part DTwo reviews each
How this is assessed

Six things

The question is testableSmall, specific, answerable with what you had today
One variableYou changed one thing and can say what you held constant
Evidence is realActual outputs — not described, not retyped from memory
The claim is boundedAll four clauses, and the fourth one is specific
Limits are honestTwo or more, about your test — not about AI in general
Authorship is namedClear what you decided, clear what the model produced

Not on this list: whether your result was interesting. A careful test with a boring result is a complete success. A dramatic finding you cannot support is not.
